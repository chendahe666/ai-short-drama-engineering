# Chapter 5: Writing Principles as Standards Proposals

A standards proposal can take the form of an RFC, a Request for Comments. It does more than ask whether people agree. It presents the problem, solution, evidence, alternatives, costs, and uncertainties together. At minimum, specify a rule ID, applicable projects, motivation for the change, checking examples, counterexamples, and a migration plan.

Suppose someone proposes, “Every approval document must be shown in full for three seconds.” The problem may be that viewers cannot read the document, but the solution has introduced a fixed time requirement. Reviewers might point out that phone sizes and text volume vary, and that showing an entire document can interfere with understanding. A revision could read: “Viewers can identify the date, version, and confirmation status relevant to the current dispute; the scene design determines how these are presented.” This preserves the goal while removing an unnecessary implementation constraint.

The comment-disposition table should record the contributor's role, comment, outcome, and reason. Outcomes may be accepted, partially accepted, rejected, or requiring new evidence. Rejecting a comment requires explaining scope and costs; the person in charge cannot use aesthetic authority to override factual errors. Retain both the original proposal and its revisions rather than erasing past failures from the record.

Validation of adoption should pass through at least three levels: someone completes a delivery using the specification; different people use the same material and obtain approximately the same problem list; and adoption actually reduces a class of errors without imposing greater costs elsewhere. The first two concern executability and consistency. Only the third concerns effectiveness. Creators saying that they like a specification does not show that it improves project profitability.

A standard must also control the cost of checking it. If one rule makes every five-second shot require three pages of forms, users may fill them in mechanically. Require a brief basic record for every shot, then add detail for high-risk shots. Whether to shorten a form should be decided by comparing missed problems, review time, and rework. Do not remove a critical check solely because “people find it inconvenient.” The specification itself needs a budget and a failure record.

This rule set is currently a v0.1 draft. Formatting or explanatory revisions may receive a patch version. When pass criteria change, record the affected scope and tell existing projects whether they need to be checked again. A new model or platform policy cannot automatically overwrite the evidence associated with an older film. Record which version was completed under which conditions.

**Exercise.** Why does “every reviewer rated it excellent” still fail to establish that a standard is effective?  
**Suggested answer.** The review may lack failure examples, use overly broad scoring definitions, or allow reviewers to prompt one another. Include deliberately defective examples, independent judgments, and review of disagreements, then check whether actual rework decreases.
