# Chapter 1: Define What We Are Actually Making

“The short-drama market is large” does not directly imply that a particular project is worth making. The same story might become a work acquired by a platform, a free series supported by advertising, a series unlocked through payments, or commissioned brand content. Each model may have different customers, viewers, payers, and people responsible for acceptance. A company's commissioned production may ultimately be watched by the public. In an advertising model, viewers do not directly pay for the film, but the producer still needs clear contractual rights to payment.

This project recommends describing a work along five dimensions: target audience, core emotional promise, presentation format, distribution channel, and revenue contract. Genre is only part of that description. Photorealistic AI, 2D animation, 3D animation, and AI-assisted live-action production also need to be distinguished. A live-action hit does not automatically establish that a fully AI-generated version will work.

ReelShort's official page for *The Double Life of a Billionaire Heiress* identifies it as an English-language live-action original built around marital misunderstandings and an identity reveal. This case can support analysis of story structure and product segmentation, but it cannot establish the costs or profitability of AI production. [GM03: Official series page](https://www.reelshort.com/movie/the-double-life-of-a-billionaire-heiress-662c25c5344cb5014203b23a)

**Worked example.** The first definition card for *Back for Revision* might read: the target is adult viewers who have experience with approval workflows and enjoy workplace pushback; the promise is “see how responsibility is shifted, then see a solution that carries a cost”; the initial format is an understandable AI-produced short sample; candidate channels still require an eligibility check; and no revenue model is assumed yet. This description guides the next step more effectively than “a satisfying workplace story, distributed everywhere, aiming to become a hit.”

**Counterexample.** Equating “workplace stories” directly with “male viewers” overlooks other groups with the same work experience. Treating every viewer as a customer willing to buy credits can also lead to the wrong business model.

**Exercise.** Write two definition cards for this case: one for general audiences and one for internal corporate training. Identify at least three differences.

**Answer guide.** Emotional entertainment and learning a process have different goals. The payer or purchaser differs, as do distribution channels, the handling of sensitive information, and acceptance criteria. Changing only the title does not establish that the two versions have been adapted to their respective uses.
