# Chapter 5: Revise Principles When Counterexamples Appear

Rules need conditions of applicability and signs of failure. Claiming that “cliffhanger endings always increase continued viewing” requires evidence across genres, media, populations, and metrics. This research round provides no such proof. Wirz and colleagues' long-form drama experiment did not detect an increase in the intention to continue watching, while some comparisons in Schibler and colleagues' written-narrative experiments found an increase in the intention to read the next installment. The materials, comparison groups, and measured outcomes differ; one study cannot simply be declared wrong. [FM09: Long-form drama study](https://sonar.ch/unifr/documents/319990), [FM10: Written-narrative study](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2023.2219456)

This project recommends writing a principle in five sentences: what we hope to improve; what mechanism we think will produce that improvement; what evidence supports it; what result would make us change our minds; and how we will test it next. When a search reveals a counterexample, first check scope and definitions, then retain competing explanations rather than continuing to search only for articles that support the original view.

**Worked example.** *Back for Revision* could test whether explaining earlier who approved v7 and v8 improves understanding without weakening anticipation. The result might be better understanding with unchanged anticipation. That can still have production value; it should not be discarded simply because clicks did not increase.

**Counterexample.** One reviewer finds the ending too slow, so the AI immediately rewrites the entire series. Another prefers a slower pace, so it changes everything back. Without a target audience, a defined problem, and a frozen version, iteration merely swings back and forth.

**Exercise.** Rewrite “there must be a high-impact moment every three seconds” as a testable hypothesis.

**Answer guide.** Specify the audience, the job of the opening, the difference between versions, and the metrics—for example, comparing how two rates of introducing information affect comprehension of the opening and continued viewing. Three seconds can be a candidate parameter; advertising advice must not be transplanted into a universal law of dramatic storytelling.
