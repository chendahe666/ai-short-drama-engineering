# Volume 6: Case Studies and Standardization Practice

*From a Production Experience to Shared, Revisable Rules*

Pilot edition 0.1 | Research cutoff: 2026-10-13

This volume teaches you how to study real cases, test production principles, and write standards proposals. It works alongside the other volumes on markets, narrative, audiovisual craft, generation, and distribution. Its focus is making an experience something that others can inspect, reproduce, and challenge. After completing the six chapters, you should be able to submit a rule with evidence, a defined scope, and a checking method—not merely a suggestion that “this works better.”

The recurring case, *Back for Revision*, is an original teaching story created for this project. Designer Lu Qiao is accused of putting the wrong opening date on promotional materials, and supervisor Han Zheng pressures her to sign a responsibility form. She uses the v7 and v8 approval records to check the facts with client Zhou Ning and discovers that the supervisor skipped confirmation of the client's changed date. The problem is eventually resolved by correcting the process and reprinting the materials, but saving the project still has a cost. This volume discusses how the case should be reviewed; the detailed script and shots are in the case files. The teaching case has not been filmed and has no audience or revenue data. It is not an adaptation of the novel recommended earlier. AI adaptation rights for that earlier novel remain unverified.

## Chapter 1: Rules Have Different Strengths

“You should film it this way” can mean four different things. First, it may refer to an external obligation arising from current regulations, platform rules, or an effective contract applicable to the project. The force and scope of those three sources must be checked separately. Second, it may be an internal delivery condition agreed by the team for a particular project. Third, it may be a conditional recommendation based on experience. Fourth, it may simply be a hypothesis awaiting an experiment. Confusing these categories leads teams to treat aesthetic preferences as law and legal obligations as optional improvements.

For example, when releasing AI microdramas in China, Article 34 of the *Measures for the Development and Administration of Microdramas* requires production organizations and broadcasting entities to add a notice in a conspicuous position in each episode in accordance with the relevant provisions. This is an external obligation with a defined context of application. “This film will be vertical, and captions will be checked on a phone” is a project agreement. “Use a close-up first to emphasize the key document” is a recommendation; it may be inappropriate if the document is too small or the scene needs to explain the spatial relationship between two people. “Showing the responsibility form in the first three seconds will increase continued viewing” is a hypothesis. [NRTA original text](https://www.nrta.gov.cn/art/2026/7/31/art_1588_73827.html)

A rule needs at least a subject, conditions, an action, a check, pass criteria, exceptions, and a version. Rewrite “characters must be consistent” as: “Within the same story period, Lu Qiao's accepted identity features remain recognizable. Reviewers inspect groups of shots and list shots in which she cannot be identified or changes without a story reason.” This makes problems detectable while allowing intentional changes such as new clothing, injury, or flashbacks.

For *Back for Revision*, first state that “viewers can distinguish what v7 and v8 each record,” then decide how much text to show. Document font size is a means of implementation; comprehension is the design objective. An automated check can report missing captions, but it cannot independently establish that viewers understand the approval chain.

**Exercise.** Classify and revise the rule “There must be a reversal every ten seconds.”  
**Suggested answer.** It is a hypothesis awaiting validation, without evidence that it works across genres. A revised version could be: In passages that the target audience finds informationally stagnant, compare a version that reveals the evidence earlier with one that retains the current order. Check story comprehension at the same time, so that confusion is not mistaken for successful continued viewing.

## Chapter 2: What to Check in Existing Knowledge

Building a disciplinary framework requires acknowledging prior work. Film, animation, screenwriting, editing, sound, production management, and user research already have their own methods. Short drama presents a particular combination of brief duration, mobile viewing, platform distribution, and generative production. Research should identify which conditions have changed; renaming an existing method does not establish an invention.

This search found a Bilibili AI short-drama course listing covering scripts, storyboards, image-to-video generation, voice work, and editing. However, the page could not be read, so the quality of the video teaching and its comments was not verified. It is therefore a lead showing that a course exists, not validated teaching evidence. It certainly must not become a claim that “the author has studied the entire course.” [Course-listing lead](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1KCgF6gEKs/)

The public PDF of T/CEAP 1—2025, *Technical Specification for Microdrama Creative Production and Content Review*, covers pre-production, production, post-production, review, and archiving. Its cover states that the China Ethnic Film and Television Art Development Promotion Association issued it in October 2025. However, the National Library of Standards search page marks the same number as “withdrawn,” and the classification scheme in the old text cannot substitute for the 2026 regulations. This establishes a precedent for standardization while limiting the document to a historical reference. The replacement text and withdrawal date have not been confirmed. [Historical text](https://www.tvoao.com/userfiles/files/%E3%80%8A%E5%BE%AE%E7%9F%AD%E5%89%A7%E5%88%9B%E4%BD%9C%E7%94%9F%E4%BA%A7%E5%8F%8A%E5%86%85%E5%AE%B9%E5%AE%A1%E6%A0%B8%E6%8A%80%E6%9C%AF%E8%A7%84%E8%8C%83%E3%80%8B.pdf) [Standards-library status](https://www.ndls.org.cn/standard/index?draftman=%E9%9F%A6%E7%8A%81)

This is also a complete research iteration: discover a text, verify its date and issuer, continue checking its current status, find a conflict, and narrow the conclusion. Our contribution should be described as “a pilot, inspectable production framework for AI short drama.” Deliver material that others can use first, then discuss whether it can gain wider acceptance. Saving, publishing, or downloading a draft does not establish industry consensus.

**Exercise.** Does finding an old standard and ten articles repeating it justify calling it currently valid?  
**Suggested answer.** No. Repeated articles may all derive from one source. Check the original issuer, status, replacement version, and scope. When status is unclear, cite the document for information only.

## Chapter 3: Bringing Real Brand Cases into a Textbook

A case card must distinguish “what was observed” from “what someone says happened.” Kuaishou's 2026 interim results disclosed that Kling's second-quarter revenue exceeded CNY 850 million, while short-drama supply and related advertising spending also grew. This indicates commercial connections among tools, platforms, and content supply, but does not disclose the net profit of an ordinary production team's work. It cannot turn “selling tools generates revenue” into “making any short drama is profitable.” [Kuaishou results](https://ir.kuaishou.com/zh-hans/news-releases/news-release-details/kuaishoukejifabu2026niandierjidujizhongqiweijingshenhecaiwuyeji)

In an attributed interview, Huang Chujie described early projects requiring multiple people, several months, and extensive character refinement. In another written interview, Jiuzhou Culture described its production at scale and identified consistency, fine control, and cultural adaptation as problems. Both accounts are useful for designing workflows, but differences in costs may reflect duration, period, image quality, division of labor, and what the accounting includes. Align the specifications before comparing efficiency. Without invoices and a complete cost definition, retain the label “interviewee self-report.” [Huang Chujie interview](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33101093) [Jiuzhou written interview](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33955220)

Platform rules also require reading their conditions. iQIYI's new “multi-episode short film” route requires no more than three episodes and a total duration of at least sixty minutes. Three one-minute pilot episodes do not qualify just because the episode count matches. Douyin's Short Drama Creator Center provides entry points for IP, submissions, matching, and collaboration; its homepage describes earnings as subject to agreement. An entry point's existence does not mean that any selected novel is already licensed. [iQIYI announcement](https://pages.iqiyi.com/p/scpr/63x0c36f.html) [Short Drama Creator Center](https://www.shortdramas.com/)

An effective case card records the project and period, audience and channel, delivery specifications, rights arrangements, work steps, observed results, missing data, and mechanisms that may transfer elsewhere. At present, *Back for Revision* can only be a design case about establishing approval evidence. It cannot enter a collection of “validated hits.”

**Exercise.** One team says it finished an entire drama in a week, while another took three months. Which is more efficient?  
**Suggested answer.** There is not enough information. First check finished duration, quality criteria, staff hours, prefabricated assets, failed attempts, and whether writing and review are included. Then calculate the total cost per usable finished minute.

## Chapter 4: Comments Need Testing Before They Become Principles

A comment is first an expression made in a particular context. One Zhihu answer that we actually read made earnings claims while directing readers toward a course; its revenue figures lacked checkable accounts. A public Xiaoyuzhou page supplied a description of a creator interview but showed zero comments. Calling the former a neutral market survey, or the latter a shared audience view, would break the evidence chain. [Zhihu answer](https://www.zhihu.com/question/1990728643920561512/answer/2015570176066736949) [Episode description](https://www.xiaoyuzhoufm.com/episode/6963ab5ce235ea65bc4bb08d)

Feedback can be processed in four steps: preserve the original context; label the speaker's role and interests; turn the opinion into an observable problem; and choose the smallest useful test. For example, “It's confusing” should not immediately become “add more voiceover.” First determine whether viewers confuse the characters, lose track of time, cannot hear the sound, or never see the crucial evidence. Different errors require different corrections.

In *Back for Revision*, suppose someone mistakenly thinks Lu Qiao secretly changed the document. The timing and confirmation status of v8 may not have been clear. We can prepare two presentations of the information, changing only the order of one disclosure, then ask viewers who do not know the answer, “Who confirmed which version, and how do you know?” Telling viewers first that the supervisor is at fault and then asking whether the story is clear measures comprehension after prompting.

Individual comments cannot establish an error rate. If five people are recruited to watch a sample, report how many of those five participants answered in each way. Do not extrapolate to “what percentage of Chinese viewers prefer this design.” The team should also preserve disagreement: someone who finds the pace slow and someone who finds the evidence sequence abrupt may express conflicting needs. The target audience and version tests must help resolve them.

When organizing comments, retain at least the version reviewed, relevant passage, speaker's role, specific issue, emotional tone, and whether promotion is involved. Have two recorders independently classify a small batch, then discuss their disagreements. For example, “The supervisor is so fake” may criticize the character's motivation, or praise the actor for making the antagonist convincingly unpleasant. A positive/negative sentiment label alone conflates those meanings. Where meaning is uncertain, retain the original wording and mark “meaning to be confirmed” rather than forcing the comment into a predefined category.

Also seek out people who did not comment. Voluntary commenters may be more engaged or more angry; viewers who leave without commenting may be precisely the people a producer most needs to understand. After a small-sample test, ask where they stopped and what they expected at that point, rather than only asking what they liked. Voluntary recruitment still has selection bias. Recording the recruitment channel and participants' relationship to the creator helps others judge where the findings may transfer.

**Exercise.** Can one highly upvoted comment saying “the music is too loud” establish that the score reduced retention?  
**Suggested answer.** No. It can trigger checks of levels, dialogue intelligibility, and audio versions. A causal conclusion requires ruling out differences such as editing and the audience reached by promotion, and specifying actual viewing metrics.

## Chapter 5: Writing Principles as Standards Proposals

A standards proposal can take the form of an RFC, a Request for Comments. It does more than ask whether people agree. It presents the problem, solution, evidence, alternatives, costs, and uncertainties together. At minimum, specify a rule ID, applicable projects, motivation for the change, checking examples, counterexamples, and a migration plan.

Suppose someone proposes, “Every approval document must be shown in full for three seconds.” The problem may be that viewers cannot read the document, but the solution has introduced a fixed time requirement. Reviewers might point out that phone sizes and text volume vary, and that showing an entire document can interfere with understanding. A revision could read: “Viewers can identify the date, version, and confirmation status relevant to the current dispute; the scene design determines how these are presented.” This preserves the goal while removing an unnecessary implementation constraint.

The comment-disposition table should record the contributor's role, comment, outcome, and reason. Outcomes may be accepted, partially accepted, rejected, or requiring new evidence. Rejecting a comment requires explaining scope and costs; the person in charge cannot use aesthetic authority to override factual errors. Retain both the original proposal and its revisions rather than erasing past failures from the record.

Validation of adoption should pass through at least three levels: someone completes a delivery using the specification; different people use the same material and obtain approximately the same problem list; and adoption actually reduces a class of errors without imposing greater costs elsewhere. The first two concern executability and consistency. Only the third concerns effectiveness. Creators saying that they like a specification does not show that it improves project profitability.

A standard must also control the cost of checking it. If one rule makes every five-second shot require three pages of forms, users may fill them in mechanically. Require a brief basic record for every shot, then add detail for high-risk shots. Whether to shorten a form should be decided by comparing missed problems, review time, and rework. Do not remove a critical check solely because “people find it inconvenient.” The specification itself needs a budget and a failure record.

This rule set is currently a v0.1 draft. Formatting or explanatory revisions may receive a patch version. When pass criteria change, record the affected scope and tell existing projects whether they need to be checked again. A new model or platform policy cannot automatically overwrite the evidence associated with an older film. Record which version was completed under which conditions.

**Exercise.** Why does “every reviewer rated it excellent” still fail to establish that a standard is effective?  
**Suggested answer.** The review may lack failure examples, use overly broad scoring definitions, or allow reviewers to prompt one another. Include deliberately defective examples, independent judgments, and review of disagreements, then check whether actual rework decreases.

## Chapter 6: Review Rubrics and Integrated Practice

This volume uses five dimensions, each scored from zero to two. Zero means key evidence is missing. One means material exists but unexplained gaps remain. Two means the material can be checked and there are no unresolved critical issues within the scope of this review. The scores locate problems; they must not be combined into an “emotional quality percentage.”

| Dimension | Evidence to check | What a score of two means |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative causality | Character goals, actions, consequences, and a knowledge timeline | Key actions have traceable causes and do not depend on characters suddenly knowing secrets |
| Audiovisual continuity | Characters, space, props, audio tracks, and groups of connected shots | Identity and action are legible, necessary information is clear, and intentional jump cuts are explained |
| Rights and release | Rights records, applicable rules, notices, and delivery versions | A responsible person has checked the material, with no unresolved blockers among required items |
| Production traceability | Tool versions, assets, failure records, labor, and spending | A third party can locate a problematic shot and its generation or revision history |
| Feedback and inference | Sample, questions, denominators, counterexamples, and revision records | Conclusions stay within the observations, and untested matters remain explicitly open |

Release blockers should be independent of the total score. Unverified rights, incomplete mandatory platform review, or materially misleading attribution cannot be offset by attractive images and a high aggregate score. Conversely, a teaching manuscript that has not been audience-tested can still be delivered as a draft, provided its status is accurately labeled.

Integrated exercise: Someone writes the following retrospective for *Back for Revision*: “Using an evidence close-up means viewers will definitely understand the supervisor's responsibility. All the material was AI-generated, so there are no copyright issues. We expect to break even as soon as the three episodes go online.” Review these statements and provide replacement wording.

Suggested answer: The first statement lacks an unprompted comprehension test and should become, “The design aims to make the gap in approval visible; this awaits validation with a sample.” The second treats a generation method as proof of rights; check input references, fonts, voices, music, and relationships to existing works. The third lacks contract, cost, and receipt information; remove the break-even conclusion and provide a conditional budget instead. A better retrospective is: “The original teaching case and review plan are complete. No finished film has been produced or pilot release conducted, and there are no audience or revenue data. The next step is to test whether the version records are easy to understand while measuring the cost of usable shots.”

The work of someone developing standards is to keep providing rules that others can use and correct. Every specification should answer which error it addresses, what supports it, where it does not apply, and who can detect its failure. A methodological framework can learn when its users can report counterexamples.
