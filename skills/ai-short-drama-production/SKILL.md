# AI Short Drama Production

Preserve the user's selected story, market, format and stage. This skill turns research into inspectable production decisions. It is not a profitability predictor or a video-generation service.

## Choose the current stage

Use existing project artifacts before doing new research. Work at the requested stage: research, development, script, directing, asset generation, editing, delivery, or feedback. Do not force a full workflow for a narrow revision. Record material assumptions rather than repeatedly asking routine implementation questions.

For a new project, establish the smallest brief that enables work: target audience and viewing context, story source and current rights status, intended platform, production form, approximate scope, resource limits, and observable acceptance. Unknown costs or rights remain unknown; this does not prevent independent story analysis or original teaching exercises.

Read [project-contract.md](references/project-contract.md) when setting up a project, changing dependencies, issuing generation jobs or reconciling costs. Read [principles.md](references/principles.md) when making story, shot or feedback decisions. Read only the relevant part.

## Research that changes a decision

Verify current provider capability, account access, platform rules and settlement conditions for the actual route before relying on them. Keep official requirements, vendor claims, creator reports, audience comments and controlled observations distinct. Record URL, date, reading depth and applicability. Do not claim access to unread video, comments or closed communities.

Use community feedback to propose specific problems: e.g. confusing identities, repeated conflict, unresolved endings, poor sound space, or unreadable evidence. Preserve disagreement and commercial incentives. Qualitative selected comments do not establish prevalence, conversion or profit.

For a disputed principle, record the original claim, supporting evidence, counterexample, revised scope, test and unresolved part. Retain a conditional recommendation when evidence cannot choose a universal winner.

## Make the story coherent before scaling media

Write the audience promise and character goals. Track causal dependencies, what each character knows, what the audience knows, and which commitments are resolved. A reaction to evidence should reflect a changed belief or strategy unless the story establishes a reason otherwise.

For adaptation, map retained, compressed, moved and added material to the source. Do not silently replace the chosen IP with a different story. An original teaching case is a separate artifact and must be identified as such.

An episode should create an intentional narrative experience. Do not enforce a universal three-second hook, mandatory cliffhanger, fixed reversal rate, single-action shot or two-to-four-second shot length. Honor the user's explicit style and duration choices as project constraints, without presenting them as industry laws. If constraints conflict, prepare feasible alternatives and resolve only the specific tradeoff; distinguish an exact duration from a maximum. Otherwise justify defaults by format or an actual model probe.

## Direct and generate with state

For each scene establish spatial relations, asset states and its narrative purpose. For each shot describe what changes for the viewer, relevant character/prop states, sound, and usable entry/exit points. Distinguish editorial shots from provider generation jobs; one-to-one mapping is optional.

Use reference assets to constrain identity and location but inspect actual outputs for posture, gaze, object state and sound. For critical text, consider a deterministic editable layer instead of repeated whole-shot generation. Choose the method appropriate to the requested look.

Probe representative difficult tasks before large batches. Accept/reject outputs by the intended use. A completed provider job is not an accepted shot. Keep returned IDs and reconcile uncertain job status before retries; record rejected attempts as costs when actually charged. Match recovered jobs' prompt versions and input hashes to the current script; an old-version result does not complete a new revision. If an approach repeatedly fails, revise the representation, split the task, change the tool or report the unresolved constraint within the agreed resource limits. When no applicable spending authorization exists, continue diagnosis and preparation without additional charges; resolve the authorized limit before issuing a new charged job.

## Edit, verify and deliver

Check causal order, identity and state continuity, dialogue, readable overlays, subtitles, meaningful sound, mobile framing and the actual destination's current delivery requirements. Creative choices require viewing/listening judgment; automated file checks do not prove emotion, legal rights or audience appeal.

Changes invalidate their dependents: a dialogue change can require new audio, lip-sync, captions and timing; a fact change can require script, text layers and later reactions. Recheck affected products without unnecessarily rebuilding unrelated accepted work.

Report separately: completed artifacts, static checks, actual media inspection, human audience tests, distribution and monetary results. Do not turn planned tests into results.

## Learn from outcomes

Use a defined hypothesis, comparison, endpoint and resource limit. Small viewing sessions diagnose comprehension; natural platform comparisons are observational unless randomization and comparable exposure are actually established. Separate continuation intention from continuation behavior and revenue from net receipts/profit.

Translate a demonstrated failure into a narrow rule revision, keep the old version and evidence, and retest affected cases. If there is no new evidence or executable test, state what remains uncertain rather than cycling through self-critiques. Persist project knowledge in its files; do not claim model-weight training or automatic perpetual monitoring.
