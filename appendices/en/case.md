# Back for Revision: An Original Teaching Production Package

[中文](../zh/case.md)

Status: The paper production package is complete. No video has been generated, no audio has been recorded, no human viewing tests have been conducted, and nothing has been published. This case is managed separately from the previously proposed novel adaptation.

## Project Brief

For Chinese-speaking viewers who understand the pressures of office collaboration, the case explores the experience of “being misunderstood → actively checking the facts → reclaiming the opportunity to explain.” There are three episodes, each targeting 60 seconds, in vertical 9:16 format. These durations are conventions for this case and may be revised after prototyping. The main cast has three characters; locations are a meeting room and the adjacent work area. Document close-ups will be created in postproduction, rather than asking a model to generate the crucial dates.

This is a sample-production plan for teaching a method. There is no evidence yet about its audience size, revenue, or finished-film quality. All companies, characters, forms, and disputes are fictional.

## Frozen Story Facts

1. On Monday, client Zhou Ning approves v7 of the promotional materials, which gives the opening date as November 8.
2. At 09:00 on Tuesday, Zhou Ning asks the three-party project group to change the date to November 18, saying, “Revise the draft first. Print it after I've reviewed it.”
3. At 09:08, Lu Qiao uploads v8 to the shared project folder, marked “pending client review.” It correctly states November 18.
4. At 09:12, Han Zheng retrieves v7 from an old bookmarked link and sends it to print, bypassing the review following the date change. The print handoff record is stored in a project system that all three parties can access.
5. At 10:00, the printer delivers the first batch of samples. Han Zheng concludes that Lu Qiao submitted the wrong file and asks her to sign a rework responsibility form first so they can deal with the issue quickly.
6. Lu Qiao does not sign. She requests a joint review of the shared records they are authorized to access. Zhou Ning confirms the date-change request in person, and Han Zheng admits that he used the old link.
7. The team withdraws the demand to assign responsibility immediately. They first stop further printing of the old version, confirm the new draft, and reschedule delivery. The printed samples and time already spent still represent losses; one triumphant line cannot make those losses disappear.

These records support the fictional team's review. The case does not claim that a timestamp or screenshot alone constitutes legal proof.

## Episode 1: Don't Sign Yet

0–6 seconds: A close-up of the sample: November 8. Han Zheng speaks offscreen: “Someone has to take responsibility for the cost of this reprint.” A responsibility form is pushed into frame.

6–14 seconds: A medium two-shot in the meeting room. Han Zheng points to the signature field: “You made the artwork. Sign the form first. Don't hold up the client.” Lu Qiao picks up the pen but does not put it to paper.

14–23 seconds: Lu Qiao looks at the sample, then raises her head: “The client changed it to the eighteenth today. That isn't the date on the new version I submitted.”

23–33 seconds: Han Zheng turns the computer toward her: “The printer followed the file. Are you saying they changed it themselves?” The screen shows only a file thumbnail at this point, without revealing the ultimate cause.

33–42 seconds: Lu Qiao caps the pen and puts the form back in the middle of the table: “Give me two minutes. Open the file sent to print and the approval records together.”

42–52 seconds: A close-up of the shared system shows that the current submission is v8, pending client review, while the print handoff lists v7. Text added in postproduction stays on screen long enough to read.

52–60 seconds: Lu Qiao says, “The printer received v7. My new version is v8.” Han Zheng reaches toward the mouse and pauses with his hand over it. Hold his reaction before cutting to black.

Episode payoff: Lu Qiao establishes a version discrepancy that can be checked. The remaining question is why the old version was sent to print, rather than another round of the same accusations.

## Episode 2: Who Pressed Send?

0–7 seconds: The version discrepancy from the previous episode appears in a new composition. Lu Qiao asks, “Do we check the versions first, or do you still want me to sign first?” Han Zheng draws the responsibility form back slightly.

7–16 seconds: Han Zheng says, “v7 was approved yesterday. I went with the approved draft.” His position shifts to the old draft having been approved, rather than denying that the versions exist.

16–27 seconds: Lu Qiao opens the 09:00 date-change request in the project group: “Zhou Ning said to revise it, let her review it, and then print.” She does not access private chats, and the scene does not depict unlawfully obtained information.

27–37 seconds: Han Zheng looks at the handoff record and lowers his voice: “The printer was pressing me. I used the original link.” The explanation emerges now; it must not be delayed until the next episode while pretending there is still no clue.

37–46 seconds: A knock comes from the door. Zhou Ning enters, looks at the two of them, then at the samples: “I'm here to confirm the new version for the eighteenth. Why has it already been printed?”

46–54 seconds: Lu Qiao displays the two versions side by side: “This is yesterday's version. This is today's, still awaiting confirmation. Let's check them together.”

54–60 seconds: Zhou Ning responds clearly: “I requested the date change. I haven't confirmed the new draft yet.” Han Zheng closes the responsibility form. The next episode must address who will take action and how to rescue the delivery.

## Episode 3: First, Put Things Right

0–9 seconds: Han Zheng turns the responsibility form face down: “This time I didn't wait for the review. I used the old link. Don't sign yet.” Lu Qiao does not respond with exaggerated mockery; she first relaxes the hand she has been holding tense.

9–19 seconds: Zhou Ning says, “We know what happened. What can you deliver today for the materials dated the eighteenth?” The focus shifts from assigning blame to solving the problem together.

19–30 seconds: Lu Qiao lists two actions: stop further printing of the old version; have Zhou Ning confirm the new draft in the same system, then have Han Zheng send it to print. The shot makes the list and the relationship among all three people readable within the same frame.

30–41 seconds: Han Zheng contacts the printer and briefly explains offscreen that printing of the old version must pause. Zhou Ning confirms the new draft. The actions may be divided into insert shots to avoid requiring all three characters to lip-sync at once.

41–52 seconds: Zhou Ning says, “Count what's already been printed and record the actual losses. Give me a revised delivery time.” Lu Qiao replies, “All right. From now on, only the most recently confirmed version goes to print.”

52–60 seconds: The responsibility form is replaced with a new-version confirmation form. Lu Qiao signs under “Artwork preparation,” Han Zheng under “Send to print,” and Zhou Ning completes “Client confirmation.” End on the form with the three responsibilities clearly assigned, without inventing an immediate promotion or a higher payment from the client.

## Two Openings That Can Be Compared

A: Show the date and responsibility form first, then hear the demand to assign blame. B: First show Lu Qiao capping the pen and saying, “Let's check the print handoff record first,” then cut to the date. Both versions retain the same main content and ending. Both are designs awaiting prototyping.

In the first round, each target viewer could watch only one version. Record when they understand the conflict, whether they misunderstand the dates, and which question they most want answered. A small sample can reveal obvious misunderstandings; it cannot establish a percentage improvement in conversion from the opening. Posting two versions on a public platform at different times produces an observational comparison, not automatically a randomized A/B experiment.

## Key Assets

P01: v7 printed sample, November 8; P02: v8 on-screen draft, November 18; P03: responsibility form, never signed; P04: date-change request in the three-party project group; P05: print handoff record; P06: new-version confirmation form.

P02 has two explicit states: `pending` (pending client review) and `approved` (confirmed by the client). All shots use `pending` until the client actually confirms the draft in E03-S04. That shot outputs `approved`; later shots must not continue to display the pending-review label. P06 is `plan` in E03-S03 (remedial plan, signature fields empty), becomes `client_approved` in E03-S04 (the system confirmation is recorded, while personnel responsibility fields await signatures), and becomes `completed` in E03-S06 once the artwork-preparation, client-confirmation, and send-to-print responsibility signatures are complete. These final signatures record the system confirmation already completed earlier; they do not mean the new draft is approved for the first time at this later moment. All states have editable visual variants, and the JSON records the transition points.

Dates, versions, and timestamps in the finished film must be checked against the frozen story facts. Chinese text on documents and in the UI uses editable overlays; future generated material supplies only performance and space. If a character is rendered incorrectly in the text, fix the overlay first rather than repeatedly regenerating the entire character shot.

## Prototyping and Acceptance

First produce three representative shots from Episode 1: the sample, Lu Qiao stopping before signing, and the two-person conversation. Check readable text, consistent identity, action that can be edited together, and natural sound. Then decide on shot lengths and the combination of models. The previously proposed three-episode sample remains the overall goal; this step prevents the same fault from spreading across the whole film.

For each attempt, record the execution environment, model, version, input assets, task ID, actual cost, accepted seconds of footage, minutes of human work, and reasons for rejection. For cloud tasks, retain the supplier ID and actual bill. For local tasks, use a local ID and resource and labor records; supplier billing may be marked not applicable. When a task's status is unknown, query the original task before doing anything else; do not blindly place a duplicate order. Any increase in paid usage must remain within the authorized budget. This package makes no spending commitment that the user has not confirmed.

This round completes only text and structural checks. The next stage must add character and environment images, actual sound and video, mobile-screen previews, target-audience feedback, and real costs. A preflight report cannot replace that evidence.
